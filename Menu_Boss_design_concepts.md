# UI Design Concepts & Creative Direction
## Recipe & Meal Planning Application for a Multi-Household Family

**Document Purpose**  
This document is written for a specialized graphic design LLM harness. Its goal is to provide sufficient context about the product’s function, users, and constraints while encouraging creative, thoughtful, and practical UI design exploration.

The design LLM should use this document to generate aesthetic directions, moodboards, component explorations, layout studies, and visual language proposals that feel appropriate for the product.

---

## 1. Project Context

**Product Name (working):** Menu Boss

**Core Purpose**  
A web-based recipe and meal planning application designed specifically for one extended family consisting of three households. The app helps the family plan meals together when sharing, while also supporting individual household planning. It emphasizes accuracy in portion scaling, food safety awareness, creative use of leftovers, and capturing family cooking knowledge.

**Primary Users**  
- Adults across three households who plan and cook meals  
- Mixed ages including children, adolescents (some athletes), and seniors  
- Occasional use by multiple people in the same household

**Key Differentiators**  
- Deep support for nuanced portion scaling (age, sex, athlete status)  
- Contextual food safety information (especially FDA-aligned guidance for seafood)  
- Tools for capturing and organizing family cooking knowledge (RecipeCombinations, ChefIdeas, leftover “decay paths”)  
- Clear distinction between shared family plans and private household plans

---

## 2. User Experience Goals

The interface should feel:

- **Calm and welcoming** — not overwhelming or overly “productivity app” in tone
- **Clear and trustworthy** — especially when surfacing portion calculations and food safety information
- **Warm and human** — reflecting the emotional context of family meals and shared cooking traditions
- **Practical and efficient** — respects that people are often cooking or planning while using the app
- **Mobile-friendly first** — used on phones and tablets in the kitchen or on the go

**Emotional Tone**  
The app should feel like a helpful, slightly wise family member or trusted cookbook — supportive rather than clinical or gamified.

---

## 3. Core Features That Strongly Influence Design

### Portion Scaling & Calculations
- Users need to input or adjust counts across multiple age/sex/athlete categories.
- The system shows live or calculated total protein needs.
- This information must be visible and understandable without dominating the interface.

### Food Safety Surfacing
- FDA-style guidance (e.g., mercury risk categories and serving frequency recommendations) should appear contextually when relevant ingredients (especially seafood) are involved.
- Safety information needs to feel helpful and authoritative, not alarming.

### Shared vs. Private Meal Planning
- The calendar and planning views must clearly communicate whether a plan is shared across households or private to one household.
- Realtime updates should feel natural when others modify shared plans.

### RecipeCombination (Complete Meals)
- Users group multiple recipes into named complete meals with notes and a 1-5 “make again” rating.
- This feature supports both planning and knowledge capture.

### ChefIdea & Leftover Decay Paths
- Lightweight capture of inspiration and creative leftover uses.
- These should feel like natural extensions of the recipe experience rather than separate heavy tools.

---

## 4. Key Interfaces to Explore

The design LLM should prioritize exploration of the following areas:

1. **Calendar / Weekly Planning View**  
   - How to clearly show shared vs. private plans  
   - How to display aggregated portion needs for the week  
   - Quick actions for adding recipes or combinations

2. **Recipe Detail View**  
   - Elegant way to surface food safety notes  
   - Presentation of leftover decay paths  
   - Make-again rating and RecipeCombination context

3. **Portion Input / MealPlan Editor**  
   - Clean, understandable way to adjust counts across multiple demographic groups  
   - Live feedback on total requirements  
   - Visibility controls for sharing plans

4. **RecipeCombination Creator**  
   - Flow for grouping recipes into a complete meal  
   - How to present notes and overall meal rating

5. **ChefIdea Capture & Discovery**  
   - Lightweight, delightful way to capture ideas  
   - Integration with existing tagging system

6. **Shopping List Generation**  
   - Clean, scannable output that respects portion calculations

## 5. Creative Direction Prompts

The design LLM is encouraged to explore the following directions:

- **Warm Minimalism** — Clean layouts with generous (but not excessive) whitespace, warm neutral palettes, and subtle food-related textures or photography.
- **Domestic Modern** — A balance between modern digital interfaces and the feeling of a well-used family cookbook or kitchen journal.
- **Clarity with Warmth** — Information-dense areas (portion calculations, safety notes) should feel calm and organized rather than clinical.
- **Contextual Intelligence** — Explore ways for safety notes, portion summaries, and leftover suggestions to feel helpful and timely rather than intrusive.
- **Family Multiplicity** — Visual language that gracefully handles the concept of multiple households while still feeling unified as one family.
- **Tactile & Human** — Consider subtle use of illustration, hand-drawn elements, or warm photography that feels personal rather than stock.

**Avoid:**
- Overly corporate or “productivity SaaS” aesthetics
- Dark mode as default (though it can be offered)
- Heavy gamification or bright “health app” colors
- Clichéd food photography that feels overly styled or commercial

---

## 6. Design Constraints & Practical Considerations

- **Mobile-first / PWA**: Many interactions will happen on phones in the kitchen. Touch targets, readability, and reduced cognitive load on small screens are critical.
- **Information Hierarchy**: Portion calculations and food safety notes are important but should not overwhelm the primary task of planning or browsing recipes.
- **Shared Context**: The interface must help users quickly understand what is shared vs. private without requiring extra mental effort.
- **Extensibility**: The visual system should accommodate growth (new categories, new safety attributes, new features) without breaking down.
- **Trust & Calm**: When showing food safety information or portion math, the design should feel reassuring and transparent rather than alarming or overly technical.

---

## 7. Success Criteria for Design Concepts

Good design explorations should:

- Make portion scaling feel understandable and even slightly delightful rather than tedious.
- Surface food safety information in a way that feels helpful and integrated rather than like a warning system.
- Clearly communicate the shared/private nature of plans without cluttering the calendar.
- Support both quick daily use and slower, more reflective moments (capturing ideas or documenting combinations).
- Feel cohesive as a single family system while respecting the autonomy of individual households.
- Work well in both relaxed browsing and task-focused planning modes.

---

## 8. Additional Notes for the Design LLM

- The family using this app values both **tradition** (family recipes, cooking knowledge) and **practical intelligence** (accurate portions, safety awareness, reducing waste).
- The tone should feel collaborative and multi-generational rather than targeted at a single demographic.
- Visual metaphors around “the table,” gathering, continuity, and care are welcome if used subtly and tastefully.
- The design should age well — both aesthetically and functionally — as the family’s needs evolve.
